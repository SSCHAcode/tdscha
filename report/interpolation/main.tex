\title{Fourier interpolation of the $q$-space TDSCHA Lanczos\\
without high-order force-constant tensors}
\author{TDSCHA development notes --- \texttt{qspace\_interpolation} branch}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a method to run the full time-dependent self-consistent harmonic
approximation (TDSCHA) Lanczos response calculation on a fine uniform
$q$-mesh that is \emph{not} commensurate with the supercell of the
stochastic ensemble, while the ensemble itself remains on the coarse
commensurate mesh. The two-phonon sector of the response lives on the fine
mesh, so a phonon at $\qv$ decays into pairs of phonons at interpolated
wavevectors, densely sampling the two-phonon continuum. Unlike the standard
route (computing, centering and Fourier-interpolating the third-order force
constants $\Phi^{(3)}$, as in \texttt{cellconstructor.Spectral}), the method
never computes or stores a high-order tensor: interpolation is performed at
the level of the \emph{per-configuration Bloch fields} entering the
stochastic estimator of the anharmonic vertices. The scheme costs
$O(\Nf)$ per Lanczos iteration, is exact at commensurate wavevectors by
construction, and reduces to designable ``stochastic centering'' kernels
that reproduce the minimal-image (zero-padded) centering of tensor
interpolation. We derive the method, prove its equivalence with
centered-tensor interpolation, validate it against direct fine-supercell
calculations on an anharmonic chain model, and demonstrate agreement with
the standard $d_3$ dynamic-bubble spectral functions of
\texttt{cellconstructor.Spectral} at interpolated wavevectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background: the $q$-space TDSCHA Lanczos}
\label{sec:background}

The $q$-space TDSCHA Lanczos \cite{tdscha,qspace} computes response
functions by iterating the linearized TDSCHA superoperator $\Ltdscha$ on a
state vector
\begin{equation}
\psi \;=\; \bigl[\, R_\nu(\qv_p) \,\big|\, a'_{\nu_1\nu_2}(\qv_1,\qv_2)
\,\big|\, b'_{\nu_1\nu_2}(\qv_1,\qv_2) \,\bigr],
\qquad \qv_1+\qv_2 = \qv_p + \mathbf{G},
\end{equation}
with one $(n_b\times n_b)$ two-phonon block per pair of wavevectors summing
to the perturbation wavevector $\qv_p$ ($n_b = 3 n_{\mathrm{at}}$ bands per
$q$-point). The harmonic part of $\Ltdscha$ is diagonal and requires only
the SSCHA frequencies and polarization vectors $\omega_\nu(\qv)$,
$e_\nu(\qv)$. The anharmonic part is evaluated \emph{stochastically}: for
each configuration $I$ of an importance-sampled SSCHA ensemble (weights
$\rho_I$) one forms the mass-scaled Bloch fields
\begin{align}
x_\nu(\qv; I) &= \sum_a e^{a\,*}_\nu(\qv)\, \sqrt{m_a}\;
  \tilde u_a(\qv; I),
&
\tilde u_a(\qv;I) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{\Nc}} \sum_{\Rv} e^{-i \qv\cdot\Rv}\,
  u_a(\Rv; I),
\label{eq:blochfields}
\\
y_\nu(\qv; I) &= \sum_a e^{a\,*}_\nu(\qv)\, \frac{1}{\sqrt{m_a}}\;
  \delta\!\tilde f_a(\qv; I),
&
\delta f &= f - f_{\mathrm{SSCHA}} - \avg{f},
\nonumber
\end{align}
where $\Rv$ runs over the $\Nc$ cells of the supercell and the phase
convention follows \texttt{vector\_r2q} (cell-origin phases). The estimator
(the ``fused kernel'' of \texttt{tdscha\_qspace.jl}) contracts these fields
into weights and rank-one updates; every term is a product of three or four
fields. Writing $r_1 = \fY\, x$ with $\fY(\qv,\nu) = 2\omega/(1+2n)$ and
$r_2 = y$, the three-field (D3) terms have the structure
\begin{equation}
\avg{\, \overline{(\fY x)}(\qv_p)\; x(\qv_1)\, y(\qv_2) \,},\qquad
\avg{\, \bar y(\qv_p)\; x(\qv_1)\, x(\qv_2) \,},\qquad \dots
\label{eq:d3terms}
\end{equation}
and the four-field (D4) terms carry one more $x$. These combinations are
\emph{exact Gaussian integration-by-parts extractors} of the anharmonic
vertices: for the Gaussian SSCHA density $\rho_\Upsilon$,
\begin{equation}
\avg{u_a\, u_b\, \delta f_c} \;=\;
\Upsilon^{-1}_{aa'}\,\Upsilon^{-1}_{bb'}\,
\avg{\partial^3 V / \partial R_{a'} \partial R_{b'} \partial R_c},
\label{eq:ibp}
\end{equation}
with no perturbative truncation; the disconnected Wick contractions vanish
because $\avg{u}=\avg{\delta f}=0$ and
$\avg{u\otimes\delta f}\propto\Upsilon^{-1}(\avg{\partial^2V}-\Phi)=0$ at
SSCHA stationarity. \textbf{No third- or fourth-order force-constant tensor
ever appears}: this is the property the interpolation exploits.

\section{Off-grid Bloch fields and the effective interpolation kernel}
\label{sec:offgrid}

\subsection{Why the naive off-grid transform is not an interpolation}

The configuration $u(\Rv)$ is periodic with the supercell: it carries
information only at the $\Nc$ commensurate wavevectors. Transforming its
\emph{infinite periodic repetition} at an incommensurate $\tilde\qv$ gives
exactly zero (a Dirac comb on the commensurate points), while the
\emph{finite} sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:blochfields} evaluated at $\tilde\qv$ is
implicitly a \textbf{rectangular window} over one period --- a zero-padded
estimate whose quality depends entirely on which periodic image of each
correlation component receives which phase. In tensor language this is the
familiar statement that Fourier interpolation of $\Phi^{(n)}$ is only
meaningful after \emph{centering}: reassigning each element to the
minimal-distance periodic images and zero-padding beyond half the
supercell, as done by \texttt{ForceTensor.Center()} before any
interpolation. The construction below performs the analogue of centering
\emph{on the estimator}, without tensors.

\subsection{The effective kernel of a windowed estimator}
\label{sec:kernel}

Let each field slot of a D3 term carry a real window $W(\mathbf{n})$
over the integer cell label $\mathbf{n}$ (zero outside one period; the
plain estimator is $W\equiv 1$),
\begin{equation}
\tilde x_W(\tilde\qv; I) \propto \sum_{\mathbf{n}} W(\mathbf{n})\,
e^{-i\tilde\qv\cdot\Rv_{\mathbf n}}\, u(\Rv_{\mathbf n}; I).
\end{equation}
Take a D3 product with slots $(z, w, v)$ and exact momentum conservation
$\tilde\qv_1+\tilde\qv_2=\tilde\qv_p$ (guaranteed on a uniform fine mesh
containing $\qv_p$; note $e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\Rv}=1$ makes the conservation
exact, not approximate). Using translation invariance of the ensemble,
its expectation is
\begin{equation}
\avg{\bar z(\tilde\qv_p)\, \tilde x_w(\tilde\qv_1)\, \tilde y_v(\tilde\qv_2)}
= \frac{1}{\Nc^{3/2}} \sum_{\delta_1 \delta_2}
S_{z,w,v}(\delta_1,\delta_2)\;
c_3(\delta_1,\delta_2)\;
e^{-i\tilde\qv_1\cdot\delta_1 - i \tilde\qv_2\cdot\delta_2},
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $c_3$ is the physical three-point correlation at \emph{actual}
(unwrapped) lattice differences, the sum runs over all periodic images, and
the \textbf{effective interpolation kernel} is the (per-dimension
separable) window triple correlation
\begin{equation}
S_{z,w,v}(\delta_1,\delta_2) \;=\; \sum_{s}\, z(s)\, w(s+\delta_1)\,
v(s+\delta_2).
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
Two structural facts follow (verified numerically to machine precision):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Partition of unity.} For the plain window,
$\sum_{\text{images}} S(\delta + L\mathbf{n}) = L$ for every difference
class per dimension of length $L$: at commensurate $\tilde\qv$ all images
share the same phase and the estimator \textbf{reduces exactly to the
coarse one}.
\item \emph{Tent bias off-grid.} The plain kernel assigns weight
$L-\mathrm{spread}$ to each image: for $L=4$, a nearest-neighbour
correlation component leaks $25\%$ of its weight onto an image three cells
away, which enters with the wrong phase at incommensurate $\tilde\qv$. The
interpolation passes through the exact on-grid values but sags in between.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Equivalence with centered-tensor interpolation}
\label{sec:equivalence}

Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel} states that the ensemble expectation of the
off-grid estimator \emph{is} the trigonometric (Fourier) interpolation of
the real-space correlation function $c_3$, with image weights $S$. If $S$
equals the minimal-image indicator (ties split), the interpolated object is
identical to what one would obtain by (i) forming the correlation tensor on
the coarse lattice, (ii) centering it \`a la
\texttt{ForceTensor.Center()}, and (iii) evaluating its Fourier sum at
$\tilde\qv$ --- except that no tensor is ever formed, the contraction with
the Lanczos vector being performed configuration-by-configuration at
$O(N_I\, \Nf\, n_b^2)$ cost. With the pre-filtering of
Sec.~\ref{sec:prefilter} the interpolated correlation becomes (a
contraction of) $\Phi^{(3)}$ itself, i.e.\ \emph{the same object
interpolated by} \texttt{Spectral.py}; the two routes then share the same
locality assumption and the same accuracy class, which is the basis of the
benchmark of Sec.~\ref{sec:bubble}.

\section{Stochastic centering: designed multitaper windows}
\label{sec:windows}

\subsection{Exactly solvable case: pair kernels}

For two-field (pair) kernels the minimal-image weights are reachable
exactly with two rectangular windows: with
$r_m(\delta) = \max(0, m-|\delta|)$ the autocorrelation of
$\mathrm{rect}(m)$, for even $L$
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12\left[r_{L/2+1}(\delta) - r_{L/2-1}(\delta)\right] =
\begin{cases}
1 & |\delta| \le L/2-1\\
1/2 & |\delta| = L/2 \quad\text{(Wigner--Seitz tie)}\\
0 & \text{beyond},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
including the correct tie splitting, and satisfying the partition of unity.
This proves that sharp centering is representable by separable
per-configuration windowing.

\subsection{Triple kernels: low-rank ALS design}
\label{sec:design}

The D3 kernel requires the two-argument target
$M(\delta_1,\delta_2)$: the indicator of the minimal-spread image
($\mathrm{spread} = \max(0,\delta_1,\delta_2)-\min(0,\delta_1,\delta_2)$),
ties split --- the separable analogue of the $d_3$ centering of
\texttt{ForceTensor}. Same-window triples provably cannot improve on the
tent kernel, but \emph{slot-resolved} general windows have small canonical
rank: per dimension we fit $K$ passes $(z_r, w_r, v_r)$ by alternating
least squares so that
\begin{equation}
\sum_{r=1}^{K} \tfrac12\left[ S_{z_r,w_r,v_r} + S_{z_r,v_r,w_r}^{T}\right]
\;\approx\; L\, M,
\qquad
\text{subject to } \sum_{\text{images}}\sum_r S_r = L
\;\;\text{(hard partition constraint)} .
\label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
The $w\!\leftrightarrow\!v$ symmetrization is realized at runtime by
averaging the two pass orientations, which preserves the transpose symmetry
of the two-phonon blocks and the Hermiticity of $\Ltdscha$. The fitted
kernels are numerically \emph{exact} for the sizes relevant here
($L=3$, $K=3$: residual $2\times10^{-16}$; $L=4$, $K=2$: $8\times10^{-12}$),
and the partition constraint keeps the commensurate limit exact for any
design (Fig.~\ref{fig:kernel}). In three dimensions windows and passes are
per-dimension products ($K^3$ passes; each pass costs one kernel
application). Origin averaging --- realized by \emph{rolling the
configuration data} while window and phases stay fixed; a cyclic shift of
the window itself would wrap its support with inconsistent phases --- is an
optional pure variance reduction. The D3 terms of $\Ltdscha$ (the mutually
adjoint pair \{$R^{(1)}\!\to d^2V$ map, $\alpha^{(1)}\!\to f$ map\}) run on
the windowed passes; the D4 terms use a single plain-window pass (shorter
range, smaller $\Nc/\Nf$ weight).

\section{Vertex renormalization $\Nc \to \Nf$}
\label{sec:rescaling}

Mode-space vertices scale with the number of cells $N$ as
\begin{equation}
d_3(\qv_1\nu_1, \qv_2\nu_2, \qv_3\nu_3) = \frac{1}{\sqrt N}\,
\delta_{\qv_1+\qv_2+\qv_3, \mathbf G}\; \bar\Phi_3(\dots),
\qquad
d_4 = \frac{1}{N}\, \delta_{\Sigma \qv,\mathbf G}\; \bar\Phi_4,
\end{equation}
with intensive $\bar\Phi_n$. The coarse-ensemble estimator delivers the
$N=\Nc$ normalization; the fine-mesh Lanczos is the operator of a
hypothetical $\Nf$-cell supercell and requires $N=\Nf$. Hence all D3-type
averages are rescaled by $s_3 = \sqrt{\Nc/\Nf}$ and all D4-type averages by
$s_4 = \Nc/\Nf$ (arguments \texttt{scale3}, \texttt{scale4} of the Julia
kernel). Consistency check on the one-loop bubble:
$\Sigma \sim \sum_{\text{pairs}} |d_3|^2\, \chi$ has $\Nf$ pair terms of
size $s_3^2\,\Nc^{-1}|\bar\Phi_3|^2 = \Nf^{-1}|\bar\Phi_3|^2$ ---
intensive and identical to the direct fine calculation. The uniform
per-order rescaling is exact up to disconnected Wick pieces
$\propto \avg{u\otimes\delta f}$, which vanish at SSCHA stationarity;
the ensemble should therefore be reweighted to the converged SSCHA
dynamical matrix before interpolating. Empirically, \emph{disabling} the
rescaling makes the anharmonic renormalization overshoot by a factor
$\simeq \Nf/\Nc$ (pinned by a regression test), confirming both its
presence and magnitude.

\section{Field pre-filtering: interpolating $\Phi$-ranged objects}
\label{sec:prefilter}

This step proved \emph{essential}, not optional. By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp} the raw correlation carries a phonon-propagator dressing
$\fpsi = (1+2n)/2\omega$ on \emph{every displacement leg}:
\begin{equation}
\avg{x(\qv_1)\, x(\qv_2)\, y(\qv_3)} =
\fpsi(\qv_1)\,\fpsi(\qv_2)\; \bigl[\Phi^{(3)}\text{ contraction}\bigr].
\end{equation}
In real space the object being interpolated therefore decays with the
\emph{propagator} range (algebraic low-$T$ tails from the acoustic
$1/\omega \sim 1/|\qv|$ non-analyticity), not with the short range of
$\Phi^{(3)}$. On the chain benchmark this produced a systematic factor
$\approx 0.5$ deficit of the interpolated renormalization, shrinking only
slowly with the coarse size. The cure exploits the structure of the kernel,
which multiplies every displacement leg by $\fY = 1/\fpsi$ anyway:
\begin{enumerate}
\item apply $\fY$ \emph{on the coarse grid} per configuration (exact
there: it strips the dressing mode by mode), rebuild the filtered
real-space configuration, and transform \emph{that} at the fine
wavevectors;
\item set the kernel tables $\fY, \fpsi \to 1$ (\texttt{prefiltered} flag);
\item fold the exact fine-side $\fpsi(\tilde\qv_1)\otimes\fpsi(\tilde\qv_2)$
into the $\alpha^{(1)}$ blocks (the $\alpha$-contracted legs also use the
filtered fields).
\end{enumerate}
On-grid this is an exact identity (the machine-precision back-compatibility
test is unaffected); off-grid the interpolated correlations now decay with
the range of the anharmonic force constants themselves --- the same
locality class as tensor interpolation. All polarization vectors and
$\chi/\fY/\fpsi$ factors are evaluated \emph{analytically at the fine}
$\tilde\qv$ (from the centered, ASR-projected interpolation of the SSCHA
dynamical matrix with the time-reversal gauge
$e(-\tilde\qv) = e^*(\tilde\qv)$), so no band-structure information is
interpolated stochastically.

\section{Acoustic sum rules}
\label{sec:asr}

Centering breaks translational sum rules --- this is why
\texttt{ForceTensor} always follows \texttt{Center()} with
\texttt{Apply\_ASR()}. An uncorrected violation is amplified by the
$1/\omega$ thermodynamic factors precisely at the near-$\Gamma$ fine
wavevectors the interpolation adds. In the stochastic scheme the ASR has a
sharper form: it holds \emph{per configuration}
($\sum_a \delta f_a = 0$ by Newton's third law and the D2 ASR;
zero mass-weighted centre of mass), with zero statistical noise. Windowing
breaks exactly this (forces balance only over the whole supercell). Two
measures are implemented:
\begin{itemize}
\item for (near-)uniform windows, the translation zero-modes are projected
out of the fields with the correct mass metric: displacements lose the
mass-weighted COM, force residuals lose the net force redistributed
$\propto m_a$ (a plain mean subtraction would leak into optical modes for
unequal masses). For the plain window this is exact and free.
\item for the sign-oscillating designed windows the field-level projection
is a rank-one modification of the effective window and would silently
change the kernel away from the designed one (measured: a systematic
$O(30\%)$ bias). It is therefore \emph{not} applied there: the designed
kernels reproduce minimal-image centering, whose residual ASR status is
that of a centered (pre-\texttt{Apply\_ASR}) tensor. Folding the ASR
constraint into the kernel design itself (it is linear in the kernel) is
left as future work; the interpolated dynamical matrix keeps its own exact
D2 ASR, and small-frequency guards protect the $\chi$ factors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implementation and cost}
\label{sec:implementation}

The implementation (branch \texttt{qspace\_interpolation}) consists of
\texttt{Modules/QSpaceInterpolation.py}
(\texttt{QSpaceLanczosInterp(QSpaceLanczos)}: fine mesh with $O(1)$ hash
lookups and $O(\Nf)$ pair map, interpolated dynamical matrix, window
design toolbox, field construction) and two Julia kernels in
\texttt{tdscha\_qspace.jl}: a slot-resolved scalar kernel and a batched
BLAS-3 version (chunks of configurations at fixed symmetry; the symmetry
rotation becomes one sparse$\times$dense product per field, the
$\alpha^{(1)}$ intermediates and the two-phonon accumulations become
$(n_b\times n_b)(n_b\times B)$ and $(n_b\times B)(B\times n_b)$ GEMMs).
The two kernels agree to $10^{-20}$; on the small toy ($n_b=6$) they are
equally fast (the sparse rotation dominates), the BLAS-3 structure being
aimed at production band counts. Cost per Lanczos iteration:
$O(K^3\, N_I\, n_{\mathrm{sym}}\, \Nf\, n_b^2)$ --- linear in the fine
mesh, with $K^3$ the window-pass count ($=1$ for the plain estimator).
Measured wall times for one full application of $\Ltdscha$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}) confirm near-linear scaling over
$\Nf = 8 \dots 128$.

\section{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

All benchmarks use an anharmonic diatomic chain defined by its topology
(identical physics at every supercell length): harmonic springs
$K_1, K_2$ per Cartesian component plus a per-bond cubic anharmonicity
$g_3 s^3/3$ in the bond stretch, in the weak-coupling regime
($g_3 = 0.1$, renormalizations of $5\dots40\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ on
$\sim3000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ modes). The harmonic bond force coincides with
the SSCHA force, so the force residual is the exact anharmonic force. The
triclinic cell has symmetry $P1$: no point-group operation can mask a
convention error, and the mesh contains genuinely non-time-reversal-%
invariant wavevectors ($\qv \neq -\qv$).

\paragraph{Exactness checks.}
(i) With fine mesh $=$ coarse mesh, the interpolated Lanczos reproduces the
production \texttt{QSpaceLanczos} coefficients to $10^{-15}$ (with and
without pre-filtering; through the batched kernel). (ii) The interpolated
dynamical matrix reproduces the direct larger-supercell dispersion exactly
for this strictly range-1 model. (iii) Rigid displacement shifts and
constant force offsets leave the fields invariant (field-level ASR).
(iv) The batched and scalar kernels agree to machine precision; the
windowed path with trivial windows equals the plain path identically.

\paragraph{Physics: interpolation vs.\ direct fine supercell.}
Coarse $(1,1,3)$ ensembles ($N_I = 4000$) interpolated to the $(1,1,6)$
mesh are compared against \emph{direct} $(1,1,6)$-supercell ensembles of
the same model, mode by mode, on the anharmonic renormalization
$\omega_{\mathrm{eff}} - \omega_{\mathrm{SSCHA}}$ (the static
Lanczos response; absolute frequencies would hide the anharmonic signal).
Table~\ref{tab:renorm} and Fig.~\ref{fig:renorm} summarize the aggregate
error $\sum|\Delta_{\mathrm{int}} - \Delta_{\mathrm{dir}}| /
\sum|\Delta_{\mathrm{dir}}|$:

\section{Limitations and outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

(i) Long-range (polar) parts of $\Phi^{(3)}$ are not separated
analytically (the same limitation as the $d_3$ route of
\texttt{Spectral.py}); LO--TO for the interpolated dynamical matrix is
phase-2 work. (ii) The fine mesh adds no new microscopic information: noise
at nearby fine wavevectors is correlated over the coarse-mesh spacing, and
the genuine $q$-resolution of the \emph{anharmonic} content is set by the
coarse supercell --- the physical gain is the dense two-phonon continuum
and the interpolated harmonic dispersion. (iii) The ASR for the designed
windows is inherited from minimal-image centering rather than enforced;
folding it into the kernel design is future work. (iv) The distributed
(MPI config-sliced) loader and the IR/Raman $\sqrt{\Nf}$ prefactor for the
interpolated class remain to be wired. (v) Off-mesh perturbation
wavevectors (spectral maps along arbitrary paths with little-group
symmetrization) are a natural extension: all required machinery
(NUDFT at arbitrary $\tilde\qv$) is already in place.

\section{Derivation of the effective interpolation kernel}
\label{app:kernel}

We derive Eq.~\eqref{eq:kernel} in one dimension (the three-dimensional
case is the per-dimension product). Let $u(s)$, $s \in \mathbb{Z}$, be the
supercell-periodic configuration, $u(s+L) = u(s)$, and let the windowed
field at wavevector $\tilde q$ (in units where the phase is
$e^{-2\pi i \tilde q s}$) be
\begin{equation}
\tilde x_W(\tilde q) = \frac{1}{\sqrt L} \sum_{s} W(s)\,
e^{-2\pi i \tilde q s}\, u(s),
\end{equation}
with $W$ supported on one period ($W=1$ on $[0,L)$ for the plain
estimator). Consider the three-field product with windows $(z, w, v)$ on
the slots and exact conservation $\tilde q_1 + \tilde q_2 = \tilde q_p$:
\begin{equation}
P = \overline{\tilde z(\tilde q_p)}\; \tilde x_w(\tilde q_1)\,
\tilde y_v(\tilde q_2)
= \frac{1}{L^{3/2}} \sum_{s_1 s_2 s_3} z(s_3) w(s_1) v(s_2)\,
e^{2\pi i (\tilde q_p s_3 - \tilde q_1 s_1 - \tilde q_2 s_2)}\,
\bar u(s_3) u(s_1) \delta\!f(s_2).
\end{equation}
Taking the ensemble average and using translation invariance,
$\avg{\bar u(s_3) u(s_1) \delta\!f(s_2)} = c_3(s_1 - s_3,\, s_2 - s_3)$
with $c_3$ evaluated at the \emph{actual} integer differences (the
physical correlation of the periodic system, which itself is the image
sum $c_3(\delta_1,\delta_2) = \sum_{n_1 n_2}
c_3^{\infty}(\delta_1 + n_1 L, \delta_2 + n_2 L)$ of the bulk
correlation). Substituting $\delta_i = s_i - s_3$ and using
$\tilde q_p - \tilde q_1 - \tilde q_2 = 0$ \emph{exactly}, the $s_3$
dependence of the phase cancels:
\begin{equation}
\avg{P} = \frac{1}{L^{3/2}} \sum_{\delta_1 \delta_2}
\underbrace{\Bigl[\sum_{s_3} z(s_3)\, w(s_3+\delta_1)\, v(s_3+\delta_2)
\Bigr]}_{S_{z,w,v}(\delta_1,\delta_2)}
\; c_3(\delta_1, \delta_2)\;
e^{-2\pi i(\tilde q_1 \delta_1 + \tilde q_2 \delta_2)}.
\label{eq:appkernel}
\end{equation}
Because the windows are zero-padded (support $\le L$), the differences
$\delta_i$ range over $(-L, L)$ and each \emph{physical} image of
$c_3^{\infty}$ is weighted by $S$ at its actual (unwrapped) difference and
carries its actual phase --- this is where the centering choice lives. Two
consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Commensurate limit.} If $\tilde q_i$ are commensurate, all
images of a difference class $(\delta_1, \delta_2) \bmod L$ share the same
phase, so only the class sums $\sum_{\mathbf n} S(\delta + L\mathbf n)$
matter. If they equal $L$ (partition of unity), Eq.~\eqref{eq:appkernel}
collapses to the exact coarse average --- for \emph{any} window design
satisfying the constraint. The plain window satisfies it identically
($S = L - \mathrm{spread}$, class sums $= L$).
\item \textbf{Off-grid.} The kernel $S/L$ is the image-assignment rule of
the interpolation. The minimal-image target of
Sec.~\ref{sec:design} makes it the indicator of the most compact image
(ties split), reproducing zero-padded tensor centering.
\end{itemize}
The same algebra applies verbatim to every D3 term (the $y$ field can sit
in any slot) and, with one more field, to the D4 terms, whose kernel is the
window quadruple correlation. Note also that no origin appears in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:appkernel}: translating the window (equivalently, rolling
the data) leaves the expectation invariant, so origin averaging only
reduces variance.

\section{Gaussian integration by parts and the pre-filter identity}
\label{app:ibp}

For the Gaussian density $\rho_\Upsilon$ with covariance
$\avg{u_a u_b} = \Upsilon^{-1}_{ab}$, Wick/Isserlis integration by parts
gives, for any observable $F(u)$,
$\avg{u_a F} = \Upsilon^{-1}_{ab} \avg{\partial F/\partial u_b}$.
Applying it twice to the D3 average (with
$\delta f = f - f_{\mathrm{SSCHA}} - \avg{f}$, whose second derivative
equals that of the full force $f = -\partial V/\partial R$):
\begin{equation}
\avg{u_a\, u_b\, \delta f_c}
= \Upsilon^{-1}_{ab} \underbrace{\avg{\delta f_c}}_{=0}
+ \Upsilon^{-1}_{aa'} \Upsilon^{-1}_{bb'}
\avg{\frac{\partial^2 \delta f_c}{\partial u_{a'} \partial u_{b'}}}
= -\,\Upsilon^{-1}_{aa'} \Upsilon^{-1}_{bb'}
\avg{\partial^3_{a'b'c} V}.
\end{equation}
In the SSCHA mode basis $\Upsilon^{-1}$ is diagonal with eigenvalues
$\fpsi(\qv,\nu) = (1+2n_{\qv\nu})/2\omega_{\qv\nu}$: every
\emph{displacement} leg of the correlation carries a propagator factor
$\fpsi$, while force legs carry none. Since the Lanczos kernel multiplies
every displacement leg by $\fY = 1/\fpsi$ (the $r_1 = \fY x$ fields), the
combination it averages is the \emph{bare} vertex contraction. The
pre-filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:prefilter} simply moves the $\fY$
multiplication \emph{before} the off-grid transform: on the coarse grid
$\fY x$ is computed exactly (mode-diagonal), and its real-space transform
is a configuration whose correlations with $y$ are contractions of the
short-ranged $\Phi^{(3)}$ (in the Cartesian basis, before any polarization
projection --- the projections are applied at the fine wavevectors
analytically). Off-grid, interpolating the filtered fields therefore
interpolates a $\Phi$-ranged object; the exact fine-side factors
$\fpsi(\tilde\qv)$ that the kernel formulas still require are supplied
analytically (folded into the $\alpha^{(1)}$ blocks and set to unity in
the kernel tables).

For the four-field averages the same expansion produces, besides the
connected $\avg{\partial^4 V}$ term, disconnected pieces proportional to
$\avg{u \otimes \delta f} = \Upsilon^{-1}(\avg{\partial^2 V} - \Phi)$,
which vanish when $\Phi$ is the converged SSCHA matrix. This is the
stationarity condition under which the uniform vertex rescaling of
Sec.~\ref{sec:rescaling} is exact.
